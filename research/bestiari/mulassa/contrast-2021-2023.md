# Mulassa: cross-performance review and simplification proposal

Date: 2026-10-19. This document records the research/proposal pass. Subsequent accepted compass, leg and expressive-motion changes are now applied to the prototype; see `simple-carriers-v3.md`. The floor-path simplification remains a proposal.

## Sources and method

- Primary: [Santa Ursula 2017, El Camp Difusio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D53pgXOeIL0).
- Contrast: [Santa Ursula 2021, Arditproduccions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WzO1q5mdPes).
- Additional contrast: [Sant Joan 2023, Arditproduccions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BaVEzBKbm4U).
- [Official figure description](https://uaf.cat/seguici-ceremonial/mulassa/): four carriers, two at the front and two at the rear.

The local copies already existed in the research dossier. Overview sheets cover the dance in 2021 and 2023; denser sheets cover the 2021 opening, turns, middle and finale, and the 2023 opening. Selected opening and first-turn windows were also played in the comparison page and sampled during playback. This is not a claim of an exhaustive, continuously watched frame-by-frame audit of all three recordings.

The review page is `/tools/dev/mulassa-contrast.html?mute`. It compares the source performances, not an updated 3D implementation. Phase buttons select approximate corresponding windows. They do not synchronize musical beats. Tempos, introductions and cameras differ; a constant time offset is invalid.

## 1. Head and body

The real head shifts and tilts visibly during the sway blocks. Some of that movement accompanies the chest and skirt: it must first be reconstructed as whole-body weight transfer/roll. The footage inspected here does not establish an independently hinged neck.

Current implementation findings:

- `tools/dev/bestiari-compare/stage.js` resets neck rotation to `(0.15, 0, 0)` and head pitch to `0.2` in reconstruction mode. It has no measured secondary head channel.
- `tools/dev/bestiari-compare/motion.js` suppresses roll by up to 90% in three fitted windows. These fits can improve a profile projection while discarding lateral motion visible from another view.

Proposal: restore a measured lateral weight-transfer channel before adding neck animation. Compare head, chest and cloth-edge slopes together. Add only a small delayed head response if relative movement is supported, and label any exaggeration as adaptation. Preserve the original head/body proportions.

## 2. Fixed directions and a simpler floor path

The current spatial frame uses the opening camera axes and explicitly has no established compass. The reconstruction has 102 heading anchors and 53 floor-position anchors, plus several projection corrections. This is too easy to confuse with measured choreography. In particular, the 70-86 second zigzag remains an inferred metric path.

Proposed notation, not yet implemented:

- Four fixed rehearsal directions around the center. Call the 2017 opening-facing direction **W** and the opposite **E**; N/S complete the perpendicular axes.
- These are **script labels, not surveyed geographic north**. Keep the camera as its own icon. Rotating the camera must not rotate the labels.
- This convention allows the first blocks to read: **face W and sway; half turn to E; repeat; half turn to W**. It does not imply a long walk toward W during the opening.
- Separate `faces`, `travels toward`, `turn direction`, and `sways toward`. Facing W does not prove displacement W.
- Start with a few destinations: center, an advance point and a return point. Fit these against stationary plaza landmarks, not the image position of the head.
- The advance after the second half turn is mainly toward the opening-facing side in the current estimate; its exact cardinal components remain inferred. Do not present a straight W walk as measured until the ground registration is checked.
- Use phrase-level actions with smooth transitions, keeping large turns and the clearly visible fast advance. Do not smooth away the dance's energy just to simplify the path.

## 3. Legs: simplify what is visible

Preserve four human carriers. Do not replace them with an animal's four-legged gait. Preserve original beast and cloth proportions.

Recommended visual change:

1. Keep hips, thighs and knees concealed inside the existing cloth volume in normal poses. Check containment at the strongest observed tilt, not only at rest.
2. Expose short, mostly vertical trouser cuffs and compact shoes. Natural cloth/body occlusion should hide the far-side legs; do not remove carriers according to camera angle.
3. Use short coordinated placements under each carrier. Avoid wide lateral spreading, crossed legs and exposed angular knee chains.
4. During turns, take several small repositioning steps instead of stretching planted feet far away from the body. Keep grounded feet planted for their actual stance interval.
5. During front/back tilt, use different carrier support heights within plausible reach; do not rotate all legs rigidly with the beast or force every foot into permanent contact.
6. Retain a minimal hidden articulation only where necessary for contact and clearance. The viewer should read human shoes walking underneath cloth, rather than eight independent mechanical limbs.

Simplifying the floor path first should reduce the collision-avoidance corrections currently driving abrupt leg shapes. A numerical no-intersection test alone cannot establish natural movement.

## Suggested implementation order and acceptance

1. Fixed rehearsal axes and a simple overhead action sequence, with uncertainty visible.
2. Root travel and heading at the shared phrase landmarks, compared against 2017 and checked against the other performances.
3. Body roll and head silhouette, checked from both profile and front views.
4. Compact carrier geometry and small steps. Inspect every turn and large tilt for protruding parts, sliding soles and abrupt changes.

Keep the original comparison available for A/B. Validate continuously around transitions and inspect the full dance before claiming visual completion. Exact final turn directions/counts, metric ground travel and any independent neck movement remain open research items.
