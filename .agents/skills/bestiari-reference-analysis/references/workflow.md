# Evidence and reconstruction contract

## Identify source and versions

Record source URL, author, event/date if established, downloaded file hash, frame rate, duration, resolution and
audio streams. Distinguish the ceremonial figure from a smaller, carnival or historical variant. Preserve the chosen
performance's exact time origin. Camera cuts, missing start/end and occluded feet are explicit limitations.

## Annotate before fitting

For every interval retain start/end source seconds, plain-language action, heading observations, travel direction,
visible support events, musical cue, confidence, and a pointer to the evidence. Use observed/inferred/adapted/pending.
Do not call a sway a counted step; do not extrapolate a hidden shoe into an observed contact. Inspect continuous
footage and dense frames around turns and transitions; coarse contact sheets only index the video.

Define a fixed floor frame using landmarks. Compass labels are optional and figure-specific: the Drac's opening
advance defines its S convention, but other figures do not inherit it. Camera position is independent. Track camera
pan/zoom using the background before interpreting apparent on-screen displacement as floor travel.

## Music

Separate audible periodicity, notated beats, phrase boundaries and authored tempo. A loud explosion or increasing
energy does not prove a faster beat. Compare score, repetitions, anacrusis, rests, holds and endings where available.
Retain competing interpretations and residual timing error. State listening as unverified unless audio was actually
perceived; waveform/onset analysis and a successful playback call are not auditioning.

## Reconstruction

Keep source seconds, song seconds and beats distinct. Use explicit monotonic mappings with documented anchors.
Same time must yield the same pose after any seek order. Keep unwrapped headings through full turns and reversals.
Use actual carrier count and figure rig. A carried rigid object should not acquire independent animal-like limbs.
Planted shoes need stable world contacts; body bounce must not drag them. Avoid per-frame allocation and accumulation.

The reference camera may approximate the recording; a fixed camera and overhead view must expose the underlying
trajectory. Do not warp the video to conceal a bad model timing fit. Label inferred metric distances and adapted
expressiveness. A source-aligned reconstruction and the current game are different comparison modes.

## Validation and delivery

Inspect beginning, returns, turns, holds, repeated phrases, acceleration, reversal and final settling. Check the
rendered model from matching and independent angles. Test seek, playback, pause, restart and mute. Verify mobile
portrait/landscape and desktop; a responsive viewport is not physical-device performance evidence.
Keep the original symptom and unresolved discrepancies in validation.md. A skill is ready to reuse when another
figure can follow it without copying the first figure's unsupported assumptions.

### Lessons from the Mulassa opening revision

Do not call a long phrase stationary after inspecting only its heading. Compare a stable body landmark against
background landmarks, inspect visible foot crops, and separate camera pan/zoom from lateral travel. When a turn
changes the visible side, a tracked cloth panel can switch: bridge that interval explicitly instead of treating
the centroid jump as direct world displacement. Pixel tracking does not establish metric distances or hidden
contacts. A plausible foot solver should preserve stance positions, fixed limb lengths and clearance around the
planted shoe during turns; validate those separately from source-foot fidelity. Keep a reproducible failing
baseline for the actual complaint (for example, an almost static opening).

### Whole-performance and carrier regression checks

Review the entire source duration with synchronized model playback. Retain actual capture times and label
intermediate evidence. Revisit intervals changed after that pass; never present an earlier contact sheet as
acceptance of later code. Inspect travel speed independently of a camera that follows the figure: a short floor
path can be hidden by framing. Check repeated passages beyond the opening; a generic sway often misses signed
front raises/dips later in the performance.

For multi-carrier rigs, test all shoe pairs, not just each person's two feet. Inspect actual transformed leg and
torso vertices against the unchanged shell. Sample between source frames as well as at frame times. Verify
both sides of every lift-off and landing: collision avoidance that passes still-frame checks can introduce
visible pops. Preserve fixed bones and planted contacts. Do not enlarge the costume to conceal a rig defect.

### Accepted silhouette and simple visible supports

Preserve an approved model's proportions, head, neck, costume and palette unless changes are requested. Separate
geometry defects from rig, animation and camera defects. The owner rejected the exposed, heavily bent multi-joint
"spider" appearance: prefer concealed thighs/knees and short trouser cuffs with rigid shoes when the reference
supports that simplification. Cuff cloth may fit beneath the hem; this is not permission to stretch anatomical
bones or shoes. Validate the actual visible geometry, world contact position, sole orientation and all shoe pairs.
Derive carrier count anew for each figure. A research gait must also be adapted to actual travel in the free viewer.

### Head motion, expressiveness and camera seams

Inspect head/body sway even when heading is stable. Reproduce observed structural motion before adding bounded
festive exaggeration. Avoid independent animal-like neck motion when the real head moves with a rigid shell.
Keep source-aligned and artistic variants available; acceleration and stronger sway must preserve contact quality.

When a user reports a bad frame, inspect several seconds on both sides. Check pose, camera position, target height
and depth, blend weights, angle wrapping and phase resets independently. The Mulassa camera audit demonstrated
that an incoming fit could overwrite an outgoing weight and jump the image while body motion stayed continuous.
Validate the projected model across blend entry and exit, including between-frame times, not only scalar curves.
Keep the failing baseline and a regression that detects the reported symptom; do not relax limits to pass it.

### Helpers, music adaptation and integration

Research each figure's own helpers and uniform with source crops and timestamps. Distinguish carriers, guides,
relief members, musicians and spectators. Four interior carriers do not establish four exterior cardinal guides.
Label a four-guide game layout as an adaptation unless documented; never copy the Drac's camouflage by default.
Record garment colours, accessories and insignia confidence. Keep seated members and standing guides distinct,
reuse the game's crowd infrastructure and check clearance against the full swept dance path.

Adapt festive music only within the requested scope, preserving the target project's sound and generation pipeline.
Retain phrase-level source/song/beat anchors when accelerating or shortening. Avoid compressing an entire reference
into a shorter song by normalized duration. Review audio by listening, with clear disclosure when unavailable.
Integrate the accepted model/rig consistently in the game and free viewer; keep research cameras separate from
play cameras. Revalidate support motion, chart timing and finale after any timing or amplitude change.

For delivery, verify the preview server's worktree, media routes and LAN address before giving a mobile link.
Distinguish local edits, numerical checks, visual inspection, listening, user acceptance and publication. Preserve
concurrent changes and retain actionable evidence in the dossier rather than relying on chat history alone.
